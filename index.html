How to Read This Tool

Results come from the relay's own record and settings. Confirm against the diagram and tabs before acting.

Reading an event

  1. Headline — the likely trip cause.
  2. Logic diagram — the trip path and any AND conditions.
  3. Flags — easy-to-misread cases (internal-logic trip, breaker already open, failed VT/CT channel).
  4. Charts — all share one time axis; trip and trigger markers line up.

Chart controls

  • Scroll to zoom (all charts together).
  • Click or drag to place the cursor and read values on every chart.
  • Drag empty space to pan when zoomed. Double-click to reset.

Reads left (first condition) to right (trip command).

  • Green box — true at the trip.
  • Grey box — false at the trip; shown only when needed to explain the result.
  • Thick accent border — an internal SV logic/timer variable. Hover for its equation; click for detail.
  • Bracket with "&" — AND: all had to be true together.
  • Bracket without "&" — OR: one was enough. Paths that didn't happen are hidden.
  • NOT / R_TRIG / F_TRIG — negated, or counts only on the rising/falling edge.
  • Red box on the right — the trip command. A note below counts other trip conditions not involved.

Start here

  • 🛡️ Protection — overcurrent pickup, measured value, and multiple of pickup.
  • 🩺 Diagnostics — close readiness, close attempt, and sequence voltage vs. sequence current checks (CT wiring, grounding, open phase).
  • 🔄 Reclosing — whether the device comes back by itself, and what it waits on.
  • 📍 Event Timeline — every bit change, in order, labeled.

Reference

  • 🔌 Voltages — pre-fault vs. fault voltage, and voltage-element pickups vs. measured.
  • 〰️ Currents — pre-fault vs. fault current per channel.
  • 🔗 SV Logic — SV timers/variables in the trip equation, with delays.
  • 📊 Frequency — frequency-element pickups vs. measured.
  • 📋 Equations — raw SELogic trip, fault, and reclose equations.

Sequence quantities are calculated from all three phases to show imbalance.

  • Positive-sequence — the normal, balanced part. A healthy system is almost all positive-sequence.
  • Negative-sequence (V2, I2) — imbalance between phases: single-phase fault, open conductor, blown fuse, or unbalanced load.
  • Zero-sequence (3V0, 3I0) — a component common to all three phases; points to a ground fault or neutral shift.

In practice: a sequence trip means imbalance, not its cause. Elevated phase current with it suggests a real fault; little current change suggests a sensing issue, remote disturbance, or inverter response. See the Diagnostics tab.

Should I act on a diagnostic flag?
Flags mark something worth a closer look; they don't prescribe an action.

Can the headline cause be wrong?
Yes — if settings are incomplete or the event spans several records. Check the Equations tab against the relay settings.

Why are some diagram boxes false?
They're needed to explain the result, e.g. the other side of an AND or NOT.
